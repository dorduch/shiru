Pip was the smallest fox in Fernwood, but he had the biggest collection of questions. One windy morning, Pip heard a tiny chirp beside the old bridge. A robin's nest had slipped onto a low branch above the stream, and the robin was too frightened to fly down.

Pip's paws trembled when he looked at the water. He did not feel brave. Then he remembered what his mother always said: being brave does not mean feeling no fear. It means taking one careful step while fear walks beside you.

Pip found a broad piece of bark and laid it across two stones. He tested it with one paw, then another. Slowly, he crossed. He climbed the willow, nudged the nest into a safe fork, and waited while the robin fluttered home.

On the way back, Pip still felt shaky. His friends cheered, but Pip smiled for a different reason. He had learned that courage could be quiet, careful, and small enough to fit inside one little fox.
