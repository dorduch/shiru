# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Shiru serves two closely connected audiences:

- Parents and caregivers who build and manage a local library of audio and video cards, import or record media, and control what is available.
- Children ages 3–10 who independently choose and play familiar media through a simple, distraction-free interface.

The child experience must be immediately understandable without reading-heavy navigation. Parent workflows should remain efficient, explicit, and protected from accidental child access.

## Product Purpose

Shiru is a local, privacy-first audio and video player for children. Parents prepare a trusted media library on the device; children tap visual cards to listen or watch without entertainment feeds, recommendations, accounts, or backend services.

Success means children can confidently operate playback on their own, while parents can load, record, organize, and maintain the library using familiar device media flows.

## Brand Personality

Calm, playful, trustworthy.

The experience should feel warm and engaging without competing for attention. Its pixel-art identity provides personality and clear state feedback, while the interaction model remains dependable and predictable for both children and parents.

## Anti-references

- Overstimulating entertainment feeds, autoplay queues, recommendations, engagement loops, and dense media catalogs.
- Generic SaaS or dashboard styling that makes a child-focused player feel administrative or impersonal.
- Tiny controls, hidden essential actions, complex gestures, and playback interfaces that assume adult dexterity or technical knowledge.

## Design Principles

1. **Child-first focus:** Keep the child-facing experience centered on choosing and playing one item at a time, with minimal distraction and obvious state changes.
2. **Parent control:** Make library management, media selection, and protected actions clear and reliable without leaking complexity into the child experience.
3. **Local and privacy-first:** Store and play media on-device. Avoid accounts, remote feeds, tracking, or network-dependent core behavior.
4. **Familiar native media flows:** Use platform-standard picking, recording, permissions, and playback conventions where they reduce uncertainty for parents.
5. **Preserve the pixel-art identity:** Extend the existing visual language and animation system consistently as audio and video capabilities grow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Use large touch targets appropriate for young children and accessible parent controls.
- Provide semantic labels for interactive elements, media types, playback state, errors, and progress.
- Maintain high contrast across artwork, controls, text, badges, and focus states.
- Never rely on color alone to communicate media type, selection, playback, success, or failure.
- Keep motion and state feedback restrained, purposeful, and understandable; respect reduced-motion preferences where available.
- Pair icons and unfamiliar controls with clear labels or accessible names.
